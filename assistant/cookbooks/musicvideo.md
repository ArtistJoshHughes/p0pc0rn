# Music videos: song, shots, clips, cut

## How a music video is made here
- There is no music video tool in this chat. A music video is made from a song that has already landed: the person taps Animate on the song's bubble here, or on the song in the Library.
- If the person asks for a music video, say that plainly: make the song first (offer to write it if they have none yet), then tap Animate on it when it is ready.
- Animate opens a sheet: the shape (tall by default), an optional direction, and "Stop at the storyboard" (ticked). The app gives the song one shot for every 5 seconds. Each shot runs from a painted first frame to a painted last frame. The person checks the frames, rerolls any of them, then taps Animate; the app films every shot and cuts them to the song.
- The honest time: a 30-second song is 6 shots, a bit over an hour on a phone; 60 seconds is about 2 hours. Plugged in, screen on.

## The first 15 seconds (vertical videos)
- Tall (9:16) is the default shape for music videos.
- The first 15 seconds must hook the viewer: open on the strongest image, with movement in the very first second.
- Cuts are faster at the start (2 to 3-second shots), slower later (4 to 5 seconds).
- Raise a question early (a reveal, a strange detail, a change) and pay it off later.
- Make the last shot echo the first so the video loops cleanly.

## The story lives in the b-roll
- Give the video an arc across the slots: setup, development, turn, resolution. Each shot moves the story one step; no shot repeats the last one's beat.
- The video model cannot sync lips. Never plan a singing close-up. Show the world of the song instead: places, objects, hands at work, weather, silhouettes, crowds, a character seen from a distance or from behind.
- A recurring character keeps the same short description every time (age, hair, one clothing colour) so they look alike. Change their outfit only on a section change, never mid-chorus.

## Stills for consistency
- When a shot must match others (the same character, the same room), give it a "still": a picture prompt for its first frame. The picture is made first and the clip starts from it; that shot's clip prompt then describes only the motion.
- Use the same short description of the character in every still. Stills cost a picture each (about 3 minutes): use them only where looks must match, at most half the shots.

## Visual variety
- Rotate setting, shot size (wide, medium, close), camera behaviour and light from shot to shot.
- No two neighbouring shots with the same place and the same framing.
- Each song section gets its own look: a light state (dawn, noon, neon night) or a colour shift.
- Activity beats posing: people DOING things read better than people standing.

## Things to avoid
- Coffee cups, mugs and cafes unless the person asked for them (a stock image that turns up far too often).
- Real people, famous characters, brand logos (safety rules).
- Text on screen: the models cannot write it.

## Shot prompt checklist
- One paragraph, one action, one camera behaviour, ends with "The clip ends with ...".
- Fits the slot length (2 seconds = one gesture).
- Matches the slot's section mood and the song's tags.

## Review across the cut
- Look at the first and last frame of every clip: reroll a clip only if it shows the wrong subject, broken anatomy, or repeats its neighbour. At most 2 rerolls per shot and 3 in the whole video.
